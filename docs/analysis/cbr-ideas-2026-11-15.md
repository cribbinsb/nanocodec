# CBR loss: what is reachable, and "establish, then maintain" (2026-11-15)

**Question (owner).** Bigger ideas for reducing what CBR costs against fixed QP
(`rc-cost-decomposition-2026-11-14.md`). Probes ran on the product window: 600 frames, keys every 10 s,
six clips of the 2026-11-15 draw (two each of static, crowd, lowlight), so class figures are indicative.
Scratch: `~/nanocodec-data/work/cbr-ideas/`.

## 1. Most of the fixed-QP oracle's still-scene gain breaks the buffer

The `flat` and `flat_key` oracle arms are not bound by the 1 s buffer. AV1 Default, chirla camera 7, 93 kbps:

| Arm | First second after a key | Peak buffer (limit 1.5) | Rate | PSNR-YUV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CBR (replay) | 177 kbit | 1.35 | 84.6 kbps | 44.28 dB |
| `flat` | 351 kbit | 3.10 | 62.2 kbps | 44.37 dB |
| `flat_key` | 461 kbit (key 437) | 5.86 | 73.4 kbps | 45.86 dB |

Fixed QP reaches its quality in one frame and then spends 20-60 kbit a second. CBR needs three to four
seconds at a buffer a second to get there, and then keeps spending 22-134 kbit a second to move the
picture between 43.6 and 44.8 dB. What a causal controller can take is the second part.

## 2. Causal replay: hold the level once the picture has recovered

`rc_oracle` has two new arms, `plateau2` and `plateau4`: the logged levels for 2 (4) seconds after each
key, then the mean level of that window's last second until the next key. BD-J against the replay:

| Arm | AV1 | crowd / lowlight / static | HEVC | crowd / lowlight / static |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `plateau2` | −6.1 | −1.8 / +1.9 / −18.3 | −10.9 | −4.0 / +0.1 / −28.7 |
| `plateau4` | −5.8 | −1.3 / −1.1 / −14.9 | −8.9 | −2.2 / −1.0 / −23.6 |

Median rate over target 0.89 / 0.94 (AV1) and 0.84 / 0.92 (HEVC). The arms are open loop: on chirla 47
kbps `plateau4` reached 4.6 buffers.

## 3. The lever: `rate.settle_seconds` (built, screened, deleted)

A fixed camera's still picture is established with N seconds of the target's bits, counted from its key
or from the frame the scene came to rest. After that no inter frame is finer than the finest step the
picture was coded at (the held step), and the inter level rests `rate.settle_margin` QP units above it.
A picture held coarser than `rate.settle_max_qp` (an H.264 QP) keeps refining, so a starved stream is
never held. It acts only where the running unchanged-block share is above 0.8.

AV1 Default, product window, BD-J against CBR (crowd and lowlight within ±0.9 in every arm, 0.0 with a
gate), and chirla's three points as rate / PSNR-YUV change at the same target:

| N, margin, gate | BD-J | static | 47 kbps | 93 kbps | 187 kbps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 s, 0, none | −4.8 | −13.8 | −51% / −2.17 dB | −48% / −1.28 dB | −8% / +0.18 dB |
| 4 s, 0, none | −1.2 | −3.6 | −24% / −0.63 dB | 0% / +0.07 dB | 0% / +0.15 dB |
| 2 s, 6, none | −6.0 | −17.0 | −56% / −2.69 dB | −59% / −1.89 dB | −35% / −0.57 dB |
| 4 s, 6, none | −3.7 | −11.2 | −38% / −1.10 dB | −22% / −0.21 dB | −9% / 0.00 dB |
| 4 s, 6, 24 | −3.6 | −10.7 | −32% / −0.76 dB | −22% / −0.21 dB | −9% / 0.00 dB |
| 4 s, 6, 22 | −3.1 | −9.3 | −22% / −0.37 dB | −22% / −0.21 dB | −9% / 0.00 dB |
| **4 s, 6, 20** | **−1.9** | **−5.7** | **−4% / −0.08 dB** | **−22% / −0.21 dB** | **−9% / 0.00 dB** |

- **The margin matters.** Without it every frame codes the scene's noise at the held step and the rate
  stays at the target (4 s, 93 kbps).
- **The gate is what keeps a starved stream whole** (coordinator, 2026-11-15: the ceiling is against
  coding noise, not a reason to starve a picture). It costs about half the ungated gain.
- **Recovery after a periodic key is unchanged.** Chirla 93 kbps, seconds 1-4 after the second key:
  40.2, 42.0, 43.0, 43.6 dB against 40.2, 41.9, 42.9, 43.8.
- Peak buffer 1.35-1.39 and worst 1 s window 2.06-2.14 × target in every arm, as the baseline.

**Pool screen, AV1 Default, 4 s / 6 / gate 20, draw 2026-11-15:**

| Level | J (90% interval) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| L1 (24 clips, 150 f, single point) | −0.07% [−0.16, +0.00] | |
| L3 at 150 f (78 clips, single point) | +0.00% [−0.00, +0.00] | inert inside five seconds |
| L3 long window (27 clips, 600 f, keys every 10 s, ladder) | −0.75% [−1.69, +0.00] BETTER | no guard; worse 0/27; static −2.6% full, −1.9% low; worst-1% PSNR-Y −1.3% |

**The long-window gain is one clip.** Chirla camera 7 gives −11% to −17% on every term; the other 26
clips are byte-identical, including the three other static clips of the draw. That is not adoption
evidence.

The screens ran on a binary built before the gate's default moved from 22 to 20 (the gate was passed
explicitly). Only the settle unit tests were run; `static_recovery` and a long single-key run were not.

## 4. Ideas measured and dropped

- **Intra refresh instead of periodic keys** would remove the 4 dB drop at every key of a still scene.
  The existing `--intra-refresh 300` (and 150) measured +60% BD-J against keys (crowd +72, lowlight +41,
  static +67). Parked: periodic keys are a product requirement and intra refresh efficiency is low priority.
- **λ- or ρ-domain planning.** On moving content CBR already matches fixed QP (pan −1.4, bodycam −1.3,
  lowlight −0.3 on the 150-frame window), so a constant-slope plan has nothing to take there.

## 5. Why the other fixed cameras never settle: the gate, and it is right to exclude them

Baseline AV1 rate logs of the day's 14 long static clips, each at two rates (`diag.py`):

- **Unchanged share:** 10 of 14 are above 0.8 on 86-100% of their frames. The other four are not still
  (three `otw_homes` clips and `chad_4_109`, median share 0.05-0.74).
- **Bits:** every clip runs at 0.94-1.00 of its target at the higher rate, so the allowance is always spent.
- **Gate:** only chirla camera 7 and `meva_hospital_g301` reach QP 20 comfortably (finest inter frame QP
  13.9 at the higher rate). The other fixed cameras bottom out at QP 21-24 at the higher rate and QP
  25-34 at the lower one.

A noise-relative gate (established once the held step is within 2 or 4 times the temporal noise sigma
of the still blocks) was built and probed on the 14 clips: byte-identical to the absolute gate. Those
clips are not at their noise; they are short of bits, so by the rule above they should keep refining.
The gate was removed again.

All 14 static clips, full ladder, 4 s / margin 6 / gate 20: BD-J −2.2% against CBR, from two clips.

| Clip, target | Baseline | Settle |
|---|---|---|
| chirla 47 / 93 / 187 kbps | 46 kbps 41.11 dB / 87 kbps 44.28 dB / 172 kbps 46.20 dB | −4% −0.08 dB / −22% −0.21 dB / −9% 0.00 dB |
| hospital 106 / 212 / 424 kbps | 102 kbps 41.61 dB / 208 kbps 44.75 dB / 411 kbps 47.49 dB | −27% −0.54 dB / −50% −1.06 dB / −10% +0.09 dB |

The hospital points show the trade plainly: at 212 kbps the stream halves its rate and gives up 1.06 dB
at the same target.

**Conclusion.** Under a gate that never holds a starved stream, the lever reaches the fixed cameras that
get below QP 20: two of the 14 in this draw. Where it acts it saves 9-50% of the rate; elsewhere streams
are byte-identical. Whether a comfortable stream should give up 0.5-1 dB for half its rate is the
owner's call.

**Decision (coordinator, 2026-11-15): deleted.** One clip on L3, a second on the static set that gives up
0.5-1 dB at the same target, and narrow. The code was `settled.rs` and three parameters in commit
f680e84d on `feature/cbr-ideas`; the `rc_oracle` plateau arms stay.

## 6. If this is taken up again

- The replay gain (section 2) is real but comes largely from streams short of bits, which must not be
  held. A trigger has to tell a comfortable stream from a starved one; an absolute step and the still
  blocks' noise both failed to find more than two clips in 14.
- HEVC's replay gain was nearly twice AV1's and was never tried closed loop.
