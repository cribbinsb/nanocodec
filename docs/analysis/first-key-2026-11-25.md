# The first key frame's size: re-investigation (2026-11-25)

Branch `first-key`. Conditions throughout are the benchmark's CBR conditions
(`nceval.fairness.own_conditions`: `--strict-buffer`, peak = target, 1 s buffer,
`rate.key_cap_buffer=1.0`, `rate.first_key_lookahead_off=1`, hold off, one layer).
"Mean frame" = target bitrate / fps. J is the dev decision score (`nceval.quick`,
positive = worse). Every figure names its clips and points. Statements marked
**unverified** were not measured.

## 1. Answer first

1. **The large first key is not an error in J: it is the better blind bet, in all six
   codecs.** Capping it at 6 mean frames costs +5.0 to +7.8 % at L1 (AV1, H.264, HEVC,
   VP9, VVC, AV2 medium) and +11.4 % on AV1 veryfast (section 4). Half the loss is the
   first second; the other half is that bits moved out of the key buy about a fifth as
   much quality in the frames after it.
2. **The refinement P frame is not the weak part** (section 5a, against an intra
   oracle): on H.264 and AV1 medium it costs 11 to 17 % more bits than coding the
   picture once, and beats an intra frame. It has to be about 3 QP units finer than the
   key it stands in for.
3. **AV1 veryfast is the exception, and it is a real defect of that rung**: with
   `av1.partial_forward=2` and no inter transform split, a large inter block cannot
   code high frequencies at any quantizer, so its P frames stop at 28-34 dB where the
   key reaches 37-39 (section 5a). Releasing the split is worth 6.5 to 8.2 % of quality
   for 8 to 13 % of cycles (section 5c). This, not the key's size, is where veryfast's
   end-of-clip quality goes.
4. **The two-step (moderate key, refine after seeing frame 1) is still not neutral.**
   On the whole L1 draw with a perfect gate it reaches +2.6 % (AV1) and +1.2 % (H.264),
   recovering 51 to 64 % of the cap's loss where 84 to 87 % is needed (section 5b). An
   idealised version comes to +0.1 to +0.3 % by arithmetic: neutral at best, never
   ahead, and it leaves the first second's bits where they are.
5. **A perfect per-clip choice between today's key and a capped one would gain only
   0.9 to 1.25 % at L1** (section 6), and the clips that gain cannot be told from
   frame 0.
6. **Informed keys are nearly as large.** Periodic keys, planned against the running
   inter quality, take a median 10.7 to 10.9 mean frames; the blind first key on the
   same points 13.2 to 14.3 (section 7).
7. **The large key does cost the end of the clip.** With a 6-frame key the last 30
   frames of a 150-frame clip are 0.16 to 0.19 dB PSNR-Y and 0.8 to 0.9 VMAF better
   (section 3); the first 30 are 1.0 dB worse. The score counts both, and prefers the
   key. This is a trade for the owner, not a defect.
8. **The own-rate buffer exposure is not the first key, and no lever tried removes
   it.** Of the 956 stored benchmark points over one buffer at own rate, 37 peak at
   frame 0. On 150-frame clips a 0.95 ceiling with the debit margin removed takes it
   from 9-16 % of points to 2-3 % at no J cost, but that pair fails the long-window
   final check (+1.4 to +3.1 %), and on long windows about half of today's points are
   over whatever is capped (sections 8, 9, 9a).
9. Recommendation and open items: section 10.

### 5a. Is the refinement frame itself inefficient? (probe against an intra oracle)

Three pool clips (meva_admin: still; waterfall: moving texture; gmdcsa: still camera,
moving people), six frames each, forced quantizers under a rate too high to bind
(`~/nanocodec-data/scratch/first-key/diag/refine_probe.py`, run on the build box). S = key at the fine
quantizer; R = coarse key, then frame 1 a P frame at the fine quantizer (and finer);
O = coarse key, then frame 1 an intra frame at the fine quantizer. Fine / coarse:
H.264 QP 28 / 36, AV1 qindex 100 / 164. Bytes and PSNR-Y of the frame named.

| meva_admin (still) | S: key | R: key + P at fine | R: P finer (H.264 -3 QP, AV1 -20) | O: key + intra at fine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H.264 medium | 49.9 kB, 37.99 | 19.4 + 21.0 kB, 35.79 | 19.4 + 41.4 kB, 38.55 | 19.4 + 50.0 kB, 38.00 |
| H.264 veryfast | 52.9 kB, 37.65 | 20.9 + 24.1 kB, 35.95 | 20.9 + 42.3 kB, 38.18 | 20.9 + 53.0 kB, 37.65 |
| AV1 medium | 48.2 kB, 38.70 | 15.2 + 35.2 kB, 38.12 | 15.2 + 49.8 kB, 39.92 | 15.2 + 48.3 kB, 38.67 |
| AV1 veryfast | 56.4 kB, 38.94 | 19.6 + 14.7 kB, 33.95 | 19.6 + 20.5 kB, 34.25 (-40: 27.6 kB, 34.36) | 19.6 + 56.3 kB, 38.96 |

- **The P refinement is not the weak part on H.264 or on AV1 medium.** It beats the
  intra oracle: on meva H.264 medium the P frame at QP 25 reaches 38.55 dB for 41.4 kB
  where an intra frame at QP 28 reaches 38.00 dB for 50.0 kB. Against coding the
  picture once, the two steps cost about 11 to 17 % more bits at equal PSNR-Y (H.264
  medium: 40.5 kB against about 34.6 kB at 35.79 dB, 60.8 against about 54.8 at
  38.55; AV1 medium +15 % and +10 %; the single-shot sizes interpolated on the two
  key points, so approximate).
- **At the same quantizer a P frame is coarser than a key** (H.264 2.2 dB, AV1 medium
  0.6 dB on meva): the inter quantizer's dead zone. A refinement frame therefore has to
  be about 3 QP units finer than the key it replaces on H.264. The first arms' matched
  offsets (+10 / +8 / +6) did not allow for that; +11 and +13 did.
- **AV1 veryfast cannot refine: a real ceiling in its P frames.** However fine the
  quantizer, the P frame stops at 34.4 dB on meva (the key reaches 38.9) and at 28.1 dB
  on waterfall, where even its ordinary P frames after a fine key fall from 36.7 to
  28.9 dB in one frame (AV1 medium: 34.1 dB at the same qindex). The cause is two
  veryfast settings together (`scratch/first-key/diag/refine_variants.py`, one setting changed at a
  time, P frame at qindex 100 over the coarse key / first P frame after a fine key):

  | AV1 veryfast variant | meva: refinement frame | waterfall: first P after a fine key |
  |---|---|---|
  | as shipped | 14.7 kB, 33.95 dB | 10.9 kB, 28.85 dB |
  | `av1.partial_forward=0` (shipped 2) | 30.4 kB, 36.97 dB | 18.2 kB, 32.54 dB |
  | `av1.inter_tx_split=1` (shipped 0) | 30.2 kB, 37.14 dB | 16.2 kB, 32.33 dB |
  | pre-transform skip, transform RD skip, regular effort, skip bias, RD trim off; trellis on | 14.7-15.7 kB, 33.95-34.08 dB | 10.9-11.4 kB, 28.83-29.04 dB |

  `partial_forward=2` codes 32x32 and 64x64 residuals from their low 16x16 frequencies
  only, and without the transform split an inter block of that size has no other
  transform: the high frequencies of large inter blocks cannot be coded at any
  quantizer. The exemption that exists (`Av1Backend::forward_policy`) covers blocks
  quantised finer than their frame (the reuse term) and enhancement layers, not a
  frame that is itself fine. This is why AV1 veryfast leans on its key more than any
  other preset (cap 6: +11.4 % against +6.9 % on medium) and why a smaller key gives
  its last 30 frames nothing back. It is the first thing to look at for veryfast's
  end-of-clip deficit against SVT-AV1 M13; its speed is why it was adopted, so a fix
  is a speed trade (section 5c).

released the first key's cap still loses (+6.06 %), so the key's size stays the wrong
lever. These are leads for the veryfast rung's owner, with one draw behind them.

texture +0.4 / +0.8, bodycam -0.9 / -1.5, videocall -1.2 / -0.9.

- So over the whole draw, not three clips: a single whole refinement frame recovers
  44 to 64 % of the cap's loss even when it is applied only where it helps. Break-even
  needs 84 to 87 %.
- What is left on still content is not the refinement frame's bits (5a). On meva
  (H.264, +11) the stream is 0.19 dB PSNR-YUV under today's: frame 0 is 5 dB worse
  (0.03 dB of the mean), frames 1-7 0.8 dB (0.04), and frames 8-29 0.55 dB (0.07)
  because today's stream spends another 9.6 mean frames on its first cadence base
  frame at frame 8 and the two-step stream, already at its forced level, spends 0.3.
- **An idealised two-step** (refinement at 5a's efficiency, the stream identical to
  today's from frame 1 on, a perfect gate): on the two thirds of the clips that keep
  today's quality it costs the key's bits again at 11-17 % (about 1.2 % of a 150-frame
  stream at a 9 % key share) plus one frame 5 dB worse (0.03 dB, about 0.6 % in rate at
  4 dB per octave), about +1.8 % on those clips and +1.2 % over the draw; the gate's
  gain is the oracle's 0.9-1.1 %. Net about +0.1 to +0.3 %: neutral at the very best,
  never ahead. This is arithmetic from the measurements above, not a measurement.
- It would also leave the first second's bits and the own-rate measure where they are
  (or worse, section 8), and the end of the clip as today wherever it refines.

## 6. The bound: what a perfect content gate could gain

Per clip, take the better of today's key and the capped key (an oracle that knows the
clip). Class-balanced L1:

| Arm | capped everywhere | oracle per-clip choice | clips that gain more than 0.5 % |
|---|---|---|---|
| AV1 cap 6 (11-25) | +6.93 | -0.90 | 8 of 24 |
| H.264 cap 6 (11-25) | +6.37 | -1.06 | 8 of 24 |
| AV1 cap 8 (11-24) | +4.90 | -1.25 | 10 of 24 |
| H.264 cap 8 (11-24) | +4.35 | -1.23 | 11 of 24 |
| AV1 cap 10 (11-24) | +3.05 | -1.07 | 10 of 24 |
| H.264 cap 10 (11-24) | +3.64 | -1.15 | 13 of 24 |

What separates the clips that gain (draw 11-25, cap 6): the key's PSNR-Y above the
stream's frames 90-149, on today's encode. Correlation with the cap's J: AV1 -0.70,
H.264 -0.60 (24 clips each). The eight gaining clips of each codec have a gap of +3.0
to +15.7 dB (14 of the 16 at 7.4 or more); the clips losing more than 5 % have -7.4 to
+8.2 (all but one, wildtrack, at 5.5 or less). Key size (r = +0.62 / +0.72) and key PSNR (-0.45 / -0.44) do not
separate them: the gap is set by how hard the motion is, and the gaining clips are in
six classes (bodycam, crowd, lowlight, pan, texture, videocall).

Consequences:
- **Frame 0 alone cannot make this choice.** The detail of frame 0 predicts the key's
  size and quality (`first_key_model`), not the stream's later quality. A cap scaled by
  frame-0 detail was the previous attempt's "best next step"; these correlations say
  it would cap the wrong clips (the largest keys are the ones that lose most).
- **A causal two-step (small key, refine after seeing frame 1) cannot be neutral.** It
  gains at most the oracle's 0.9-1.25 % and pays the refinement's shortfall on the
  other two thirds of the clips, which lose 10.1 % (AV1) and 10.4 % (H.264) each on
  average at cap 6. Break-even needs 87 % / 84 % of that recovered; measured 50-73 %
  (3 clips), and the first frame's own loss cannot be recovered at all.
- **The start-up lookahead (frame 1 read first) is the mechanism that can.** It is off
  under the benchmark's zero-lookahead rule; its measured worth there was 1.3-3.0 %
  PSNR-YUV (benchmark-procedure response B2). That is the same order as the oracle.

## 7. Periodic keys

"Why would what works for one 5 second bit of video be different for the next?" It is
not very different. A periodic key is planned at the running inter quality
(`rate.key_quality_match`), so it is not blind. Anchor probe points, draw 11-25 (6 clips
x 2 rungs, 600 f, keys every 150): periodic keys take a median 10.7 (H.264) / 10.9
(AV1) mean frames (p10 7.8, p90 27) and land 3.0 / 1.7 dB above the ten P frames
before them; the first key on the same points takes 13.2 / 14.3. Twelve points per
codec: a small sample. The 1.0-buffer cap for periodic keys was adopted at both key
shares on 2026-11-22/23 (ledger `key-cap-1-0-at-both-key-shares`).

## 8. The own-rate buffer exposure

Own-rate peak = `cbr_stats.own_rate_fullness`: a 1 s bucket drained at the stream's
achieved rate. Stored run `bench/bp3r-ours-cbr`, 35 families, 13 055 points
(`scratch/first-key/an3.py`, `an4.py`):

- 956 points (7.3 %) are over one buffer at own rate; per family 3.8 to 13.7 %; worst
  1.75 (AV1 slower).
- Where the peak is: frame 0 for 37 of them (3.9 %); within the first second for 560
  (59 %); later for 396 (41 %), typically at a cadence base frame at frames 40-100
  with the target-rate bucket at 0.95-1.00.
- Achieved / target: median 0.984, p5 0.962, p1 0.944. With the target-rate bucket at
  L buffers at frame t of a stream that achieves a of its target, the own-rate peak
  is about (30 L + (1 - a) t) / (30 a) at 30 fps: 1.00 buffers at frame 72 with
  a = 0.97 reads 1.10.
- Long windows are worse: H.264 medium anchor, probe points (600/900 f, periodic keys):
  10 of 24 over one buffer, worst 2.12 (a roundabout clip at 0.96 of its target whose
  later seconds are cheaper than its first). Capping the first key at 6 left 9 of 24,
  worst 2.01.
- First-key arms and the L1 count over one buffer (24 points): AV1 today 2, cap 4: 1,
  cap 6: 1, cap 6 + frame at +8: 5, cap 6 + frame at +11: 10, cap 8 + frame at +6: 5.
  "Moderate I + heavy P" has the same first second and a lower achieved rate, so it is
  worse on this measure.

So the measure penalises a front-loaded stream that then undershoots, wherever the
front-load sits. It is reduced by a ceiling under one buffer or by landing on the
target, not by the key's size. Section 9 measures both.

  from 62.7 to 54.4 mean frames; HEVC `chirla_seq_024` 1.007 -> 0.901). A still clip

  removes are not spent later, so the own rate drops and the same peak reads higher

## 10. Recommendation and what remains open

### What I recommend

1. **Keep the first key's sizing.** No design in the space the owner described is
   neutral; section 5b's bound and section 6's oracle say none can be without
   lookahead. The price of a first key in the references' range is +5 to +8 % J at 150
   frames in every codec and +1.2 to +3.5 % at 600-900 frames.
2. **Treat first second against end of clip as an owner trade** (sections 3 and 4). If
   the steady state matters more than the first second, the lever is
   `rate.key_cap_buffer_first`, at that cost.
3. **Hand section 5a / 5c to the AV1 veryfast rung**: its P frames' transform ceiling
   is the measured cause of what a smaller key was hoped to fix.
4. **Land the one-line ceiling rule** (identity 387/387). Whether the benchmark record
   then uses ceiling 0.95 with debit margin 0 is a condition change and the owner's
   call: on the benchmark's 150-frame windows it removes most of the own-rate
   exposure for nothing; on long windows it costs 1.4 to 3.1 % and more points pass
   their target.
5. **Do not land** the key group, any refinement arm, the deferred repayment, or a
   lower periodic-key cap.

### Open

- The own-rate measure at 600-900 frames flags 10 of 24 H.264 medium probe points
  (worst 2.12) on streams whose target-rate bucket never passes 1.00. The peaks sit on
  periodic keys of about one buffer in streams whose rate changes between 150-frame
  segments (0.81 to 1.10 of the target on one clip). A ceiling does not address that;
  the periodic keys' cap (`rate.key_cap_buffer`, 1.0 since 11-22 for J) and the
  measure's window both bear on it. Not investigated further.
- Whether the start-up lookahead could return under a fairness rule (one frame of
  delay, declared) is the only route found to the oracle's 0.9-1.25 %.
- The refinement efficiency figures rest on three clips.
- AV1 veryfast's key costs 11-12 P frames of CPU (coordinator, 11-25). A smaller key
  was not measured for speed here, and nothing on this branch changes the key's search.
  Section 3 says the size is the wrong lever for it on quality: a cheaper search at the
  same size is the open question.
