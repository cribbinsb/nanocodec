How to read this comparison +

Quality controls
Target bitrate uses realtime feedback and a configurable buffer. Set both targets equally and let the controller settle before comparing. Peak rate limits bursts over the buffer interval, not every individual frame. Fixed quantizer remains available; QP scales differ by codec.

Bitrate and timing
Each version has its own resolution and frame rate; bitrate is normalized to that version’s frame rate. A version at a lower rate codes every second, third, fifth or sixth tick of the 30 fps source timeline (15, 10, 6 or 5 fps) and holds its picture in between; Pause and Step stop both on an instant both coded. Encode time includes placing the source in the version’s size (a video’s own YUV planes; RGB-to-YUV conversion for the test scene); decode time is the decoder alone. With a GPU, frames are converted to RGB for display by WebGL. PSNR-YUV compares each decoded frame with the source frame given to that version, at its own size. Each version encodes and decodes in separate workers on overlapping frames; the busiest of the four sets the delivered frame rate, named after it as “limited by”.

Pre-encoded streams
To compare with another encoder, press “Reference encoder” at the top of a version, pick a stream (listed by encoder, for the clip playing), then “Match nanocodec to this stream” to set the other version to the same codec, size, frame rate and bitrate. A version can instead play a stream coded offline by another encoder (x264, openh264, x265, SVT-AV1, libaom, libvpx, NVENC) at the real-time settings the project’s charts use (one thread, low delay, CBR); these mature, general-purpose encoders may do better at other settings. The stream is fetched when chosen and decoded here by nanocodec’s WASM decoder. Its panel names the encoder, version and exact command, and “Match this in the other version” sets the other version to the same size, frame rate and bitrate live.

Your files stay here
Video and camera frames are processed locally. Downloads contain the real compressed stream: IVF for AV1/VP9/AV2, Annex-B for H.264/HEVC/VVC. Each recording is bounded to 16 MiB.
