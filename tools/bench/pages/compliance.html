Rate control

How every encoder's CBR streams use the shared 1 s buffer. Each point's frame sizes, every counted bit, are replayed through one leaky bucket the same way for every encoder; nothing is re-encoded. Every point counts in the BD; these figures sit beside it, with no threshold.

  • The bucket: one second of the rate it drains at, with the decoder starting after a full second of buffering (the shared 1.0 s initial fullness). Its peak is in buffers: 1.0 is one full second of bits queued; above 1.0 a receiver with that buffer would have overflowed or stalled.
  • At the stream's own rate: drained at the point's achieved average rate. The charts place each point at its achieved rate, so this is the buffer at the rate it is judged at, and an encoder cannot look calmer by undershooting its target.
  • At the target rate: drained at the requested rate, as a CBR receiver provisioned for the target sees it; an undershooting encoder gets more than one second of its own rate here.
  • What each encoder is asked for: the same target, a 1 s buffer, peak rate equal to the target, no frame dropping on the main rows (the '+drop' rows are libvpx, libaom and openh264 as their projects ship them, dropping frames; a dropped frame shows the previous picture). Ours runs --strict-buffer (it re-codes a frame that would pass one buffer at the target). The exact commands are in the procedure document (section 17).
